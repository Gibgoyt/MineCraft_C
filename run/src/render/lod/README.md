# run/src/render/lod

**Purpose:** builds the far field's coarse grids on workers and sends them to the card: each a level of voxels of 2 to 64 blocks, in a table that follows the eye.
**Tier:** Arena. Takes what it holds from the arena its parent gives it, once. Each column's working memory is its worker's Job arena. The levels' blocks on the card are the Owner's.
**Owns:** nothing of its own. In its arena: the jobs, what each has encoded, and each level's host copy of its table.
**Lifetime:** from `render_open_lod` to `render_close_lod`, within one world's frame.
**Threads:** the `gpu` thread for everything here, or whichever one thread a run has. The columns are made on workers, which read the terrain and, through an overlay if one is set, what is saved, and touch nothing but their own job and staging until `done`.
**Public functions** (`run/include/render/lod/index.h`):

- `render_lod_bands`, `render_lod_plan_by_bands`: where each level may start for a pixel error, field of view and picture height, and the levels whose bands start inside the render distance: the far field's plan (plan 009 phases 03 and 04).
- `render_lod_band_base`, `render_lod_block_radius` (plan 009 phase 04): the frame's `traverse_world_t.band_base` for a pixel error, field of view and rows; and how far the game holds blocks, d_1 and `block_margin_chunks`, within `block_radius_min_chunks` and `block_radius_max_chunks` and the render distance.
- `render_lod_octants_set`, `render_lod_octant_arena_bytes`, `render_lod_jobs_hold`: levels made from the blocks through `render/octant`, and workers held.
- `render_lod_table_bytes`, `render_lod_pool_bytes`, `render_lod_arena_bytes`: sizes for the Owner.
- `render_lod_cells_from_column`: one sampled column to its coarse voxels, on its own for a test to hold to paper.
- `render_lod_sample_terrain`: a sample of the world's own ground, its context a `terrain_t`, with `terrain_climate_fallback`'s climate.
- `render_lod_sample_world`: the same for the game, its context a `render_lod_world_t` (terrain and climate): each far column's climate sample is filled once, at the column's middle, into the sample scratch after the feature cache (`climate_terrain_sample`: tiles where built, the fallback past them). Plan 006 phase 02; nothing reads it yet.
- `render_lod_fine_set`: level 0's buffers, set by `run/src/render/` as it opens the far field: what the levels near the eye are made from, and so that a far column over loaded blocks nearer than any ray of its level starts waits until every other is asked for.
- `render_lod_overlay_set`: what is written over each sample, with its own scratch from the job's arena.
- `render_lod_column_changed`, `render_lod_area_changed`, `render_lod_all_changed`: a column is made again, in each level, with the old one shown until the new one has come. One a worker has begun goes round again when it is done, as it may already have read past what changed.
- `render_lod_init`, `render_lod_deinit`: a pair.
- `render_lod_quiesce`, `render_lod_level_matching`, `render_lod_level_adopt`: for `render_replan_lod` in `run/src/render/`, a level that stays the same when the levels change is taken over by the new far field, with its blocks, its table on the card and every column it has built.
- `render_lod_frame`: one frame's worth: follows the eye, sends what is finished, up to a budget, and asks for what is next. Never waits.
- `render_lod_idle`: whether all that is wanted is on the card.
- `render_lod_scene`: what this frame's kernels read.
- `render_lod_column_of`: which of a level's columns a place is in.

**May be called by:** `run/src/render/`, which starts and stops it, `run/src/game/`, which calls `render_lod_frame`, and `run/src/render/frame/`, which calls `render_lod_scene` and sets `pixel_error`'s bands' `band_base` on it each frame.
**Depends on:** `arena`, `assert`, `log`, `profiler` (a `far_column` scope on the worker's lane for each column), `job`, `time`, `terrain` and its stages, `terrain/feature`'s cache, `tables/blocks`, `world/column`, `render/encode`, `render/upload` for its budget, `render/octant` (its sibling: the octants the pyramid's levels are made from), `gpu_bridge/world_buffers`, `gpu_bridge/lod`, `gpu_bridge/shade` for its one light value.

## The far field is the pyramid (plan 009 phases 03 and 04)

The far field is planned by pixel error. One pixel is θ = 2 tan(half the field of view) / rows, a level of voxels of s blocks moves a surface by s / 2 blocks, so it may start at d = s / (2 E θ), never inside the near guarantee (`compile/config/render/lod/index.yaml`: `pixel_error`, `near_guarantee`, `level_count`). At E = 2, 70° and 1080 rows level 1 starts at 385.6 blocks and level 2 at 771.2. `render_lod_plan_by_bands` keeps each level whose band starts inside the render distance, each `ceil(far edge / its column) + margin` columns each way, up to `radius_columns_max`: so at render distance 64 level 1 is 27 columns each way and level 2 18. The levels' squares are planned when the view is worked out; the bands rays use are worked out each frame from the camera (`cuda/src/lod/README.md`), so a change of field of view alone moves where each level starts at once, and a narrower view meanwhile reaches a level's edge sooner and goes on to the next.

The game holds blocks (level 0) out to `render_lod_block_radius`: d_1 and `block_margin_chunks` (2) more, at least `block_radius_min_chunks` (12), at most `block_radius_max_chunks` (48) and the render distance. At E 2, 70° and 1080 rows that is 27 chunks; at E 8 the floor, 12. `game_view_work_out` in `run/src/game/view/` works it out, and the map's view radius follows it.

**Where the blocks are loaded, levels come from them.** A pyramid column of voxels of 2 stands on 2 x 2 chunks. When all four are on the card, with every octant `render/octant` holds of them, the gpu thread puts the column together from those octants, encodes it with `render_encode_section` and sends it as a worker's column would be sent, in the same frame, up to `octant_columns_per_frame` columns and `lod_frame_bytes` (`compile/config/render/budget/index.yaml`). Doing it there costs no worker and no copy of the blocks; `bench/lod/` has what one costs. It then reduces each of its sections to its octant of level 2 and keeps those, so level 2 follows from level 1 the same way, up to `octant_levels` levels. A column the four do not all stand under is sampled from terrain on workers as the far field's are, with the save's overlay; a column made from terrain where octants have since come is made again from them, and a level 1 column made again from terrain marks the level 2 column over it.

**An edit.** The upload keeps each sent section's octant, and the store lists the chunk as changed; the pyramid takes the list at the start of its frame and marks each level 1 column over one stale. The octant pass goes finest level first, so the level 2 column over it is made in the same frame: an edit reaches levels 1 and 2 in the upload round that takes it to level 0 (`run_render_distance_live` holds it to 100 ms).

`render_open_lod` makes levels from the blocks only when it is given the radius the game holds (`block_radius`); with 0, as `render_feature_lod` opens it, every column is sampled. `render_lod_jobs_hold` stops columns going to workers while held; nothing holds it since phase 04 made the pyramid the far field. `render_lod_idle` counts octant columns left for want of a frame's room.

## Why it is the way it is

**A level is a world.** A coarse column is 16 x 16 voxels and up to 768 high (1536 blocks in voxels of 2), in the same sections, table and slab pool as the blocks', so `render_encode_section` writes it, `world_buffers` holds it, `traverse_trace` walks it, and its uniform sections and masks do for it what they do for the world. Only the meaning of a voxel is different: it is `1 << shift` blocks each way. A section of air costs nothing and buried stone 24 bytes, and a level costs what its surface costs.

**One sample for each voxel's column.** One column of blocks at a voxel's middle, 256 of them for a coarse column, by the stages of `terrain_generate_box` that show from far off: no caves or ores, and what grows only for voxels of up to `1 << RENDER_LOD_FEATURES_SHIFT_MAX` blocks.

**What a column costs.** Before plan 005 phase 14 a column at voxels of 2, 4 and 8 took about 0.4 s of one worker, and a far field at render distance 64 held all 16 workers for minutes, starving everything else. `bench/lod/` found why: not the 1536 layers, which cost about 8 ms a column with nothing growing, as the density already skips its 3D noise outside twice the softness, but what grows, which walked the 9 chunks round each of a column's 256 lines again for every line. The chunks are now kept in a cache in the job's scratch, `terrain_feature_fill_box_cached`, each loaded and walked once a column, for the same bytes: 14 ms at voxels of 2 and 24 ms at 4. At voxels of 8 it was still 59 ms, with 100 chunks to walk, so that level grows nothing now and costs 8 ms. Working out only the layers the 2D height estimate allows was tried, and saved nothing: the estimate's own columns cost what the skipped layers did. `tests/run/render/lod/` holds the cache to the bytes without it.

Nothing about the terrain is written for this: a voxel is a pure function of the terrain and its place, so any worker gets the same bytes, and the far field is the world's own ground and not something like it. It also means one tree in a voxel of 64 can be sampled or not. That is what the half rule in `render_lod_cells_from_column` is for: a voxel is a block if at least half of what the sample has in its height is, so a level's ground is at the height nearest the truth, and thin things go. Ice is the exception, as a frozen sea is thin and wide: where the half rule takes ice off water, the water below shows as ice, and a level too coarse for what grows still freezes a cold biome's water, by `render_lod_sample_freeze`. Since 256 blocks below `z = 0` is a power of two, the sea's surface is on a voxel's edge at every level.

**A level never shows a hole.** Rays are let into a square around the eye's column with every column in it built, `shown_side` wide, and past that they go to the next level out, which is why a level that is still filling makes the picture coarser and not wrong. The table stays `RENDER_LOD_MARGIN_COLUMNS` wider than that square each way, so the columns an eye steps towards are built before it gets there and what is shown does not shrink when it moves: only the one new row is asked for.

**Scrolling.** As in the world's map, a column's slot depends only on its coordinates modulo the table's side, so a move touches what left and what came. What left goes back to the pool at once. A column on a worker when the window moves is dropped when it finishes if it has left the window, and otherwise still goes where it was going. Columns are queued at `JOB_PRIORITY_FAR`, behind everything nearer, on at most half the workers while anything nearer is queued, running, or was in the last 100 ms (`run/src/job/README.md`). One still queued when the eye moves to another column, or when the far field changes or closes, is dropped with `job_priority_drop`: its job returns at once, and the column is asked for again, nearest the new place first, if it is still in the window. A change of render distance or of the bands drops what is queued too, in `render_lod_quiesce`, so that it waits only for what is on a worker; the levels that stay keep what they have built (below).

**Order.** The coarsest level first, nearest column first: the horizon comes first, and finer levels sharpen what is already there. The coarsest level stands round the eye too, so a column of level 0 still on its way is drawn as it, coarse, and never as nothing: a primary ray entering a column level 0 does not hold hands over to the far field there. A column whose chunks level 0 all holds (`render_lod_fine_set`) and which lies wholly nearer than any ray of its level starts (its `d` by the last frame's `band_base`, less the dither) is covered: put off until every other has been asked for, and counted towards a level's shown square while it stays covered, as no ray of that level goes there: so after render distance 32 to 64 the new horizon is not held up behind columns nobody can see. This order was the first one, and stopped working when columns cost 1 s each: at render distance 64 the first second showed only sky, and the horizon took minutes. With columns of 8 ms at the coarsest levels it fills in well under a second (plan 005 phase 14), which is why voxels of 8 no longer grow anything (`RENDER_LOD_FEATURES_SHIFT_MAX`).

**When the levels change.** A change of render distance or of the bands is `render_replan_lod`: each level whose shift and radius stay the same keeps its columns, shown as they were, and only the levels that are new are built, nearest first. So a far column at the old setting stays until its own replacement is there. With `render_lod_frame` on the gpu thread nothing in the sim knows about any of this, and the map is not touched.

**Budget.** What one frame sends is held to `RENDER_LOD_FRAME_BYTES`. A column that finds no room in its pool is given up on, once and with a warning, until it leaves the window: what is shown stops at it.

## What it does not do

On its own it draws no edits: the far field is what terrain generates. A world with a save sets `render_lod_overlay_set` to `save_world_far_line`, through `run/src/game/`, so what is saved is written over each sample. Until an edit is captured, only level 0 and the levels made from its blocks draw it: with a far field open, `run/src/game/` captures what is edited every second, so a mined block is gone from the far field too within about a second, and a column of it is cheap to make again.
